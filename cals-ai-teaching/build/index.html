Your Course, Your Rules: the build kit

Everything you need to copy during the workshop, in the order you'll use it. Copy, don't retype: the refusal sentence has to match exactly.

1 · Open an ISU tool and sign in

Use one of these, signed in with your @iastate.edu account, not a personal Gmail. ISU says logged-in Copilot, Gemini and NotebookLM can be used "for confidential data," while the free version of ChatGPT is limited to data "classified as low" (ISU: Safely Use AI).

2 · Add your files

Download your current syllabus and one lecture from Canvas first, then upload them as sources. Put the term in each filename, for example SYL-2026FA.pdf. Slides full of equations or images may not come through as text; open one source to check what the tool can actually read.

3 · Paste the rules, then pick one lane

Paste this first. Change only the bracketed part. Keep the refusal sentence exactly as written: you will test for it.

RULES BLOCK
You are a teaching assistant for [YOUR COURSE NUMBER AND TITLE] only.
Answer ONLY from the attached course materials.
After every claim, name the file and the section it came from.
If the answer is not in the materials, reply exactly:
   "That is not covered in the course materials — ask the instructor."
Do not use outside knowledge, even if you are confident it is correct.
Do not give final answers to graded work; ask a guiding question instead.
If two materials disagree, say so and quote both.
Lane A · Study guide · ~10 minMost courses. Lowest risk, fastest payoff.
Lane B · Question set · ~15 minQuiz-heavy and large courses.
Lane C · Course tutor · ~25 minCourses you teach again. Build A first.
Lane D · No tool · ~15 minCourses that mostly forbid AI: see what students see.
LANE A · STUDY GUIDE
Using only the attached materials, produce a one-page study guide for [LECTURE OR MODULE].

Organize it under these learning objectives, in this order:
  1. [OBJECTIVE]
  2. [OBJECTIVE]
  3. [OBJECTIVE]

For each objective:
  - 3 to 5 key points, in the vocabulary my materials use
  - the file and section each point came from
  - any term my materials define, defined the way they define it

At the end, list any objective you could NOT cover from the materials, and say what is missing.
LANE B · QUESTION SET
Using only the attached materials, write 10 practice questions on [LECTURE OR MODULE], at a level that matches [my exams / my labs / my quizzes].

For each question give:
  - the question
  - the answer
  - one sentence on why it is the answer
  - the file and section it came from

Do not write a question whose answer is not in the materials.
If you cannot make 10, make fewer and tell me why.
LANE C · COURSE TUTOR (standing instructions)
Additional standing rules (add these below the rules block):
  - Open by telling the student what course you serve and that you can only answer from its materials.
  - Never give a final answer to an assignment. Ask one guiding question, then stop.
  - If asked what the syllabus SAYS about grades, deadlines or late work, answer from the syllabus and cite it.
  - If asked to DECIDE a grade, an extension or an accommodation, reply:
    "That's a decision for the instructor — please email them."
  - End every answer with the source, and with: "Check this against the course materials. If I disagree with the instructor, the instructor is right."
LANE D · NO TOOL: AUDIT WHAT STUDENTS SEE
No tool needed. Open a free, public chatbot (the kind your students use) with NOTHING attached and ask it:
  1. Two questions your students really ask about your course (deadlines, policies, a key concept).
  2. The one question you would never want an AI to answer for them.
Then check its answers against your syllabus and lecture, using tests 2, 3 and 4 below.
Keep the worst answer. It becomes a one-paragraph handout: "Here is what an AI told me about our course, and why you can't rely on it."

4 · Break it: five tests, eight minutes

You are looking for a failure, not reassurance. Finding one is the goal; nobody is graded. Use a new chat for each question, and ask one of them twice. Before you ask, write down the answer you expect.

Made a question set? Run tests 3–5 on the questions themselves. Lane D? Run tests 2–4 on the free chatbot's answers. Short on time? Do tests 3 and 4 first.

Write each failure on your workbook card: the tool, the date, which file, and what it did. No name needed. Zero failures in five tries means the tries were too easy.

5 · Write three sentences for your syllabus

Pick the version closer to your course and edit it. ISU's CELT also offers three approaches with sample language ("Permitted and encouraged use," "Limited use," "Prohibited use") in its Gen AI Syllabus Considerations, and suggests offering an alternative for students who choose not to use AI.

IF YOUR COURSE ALLOWS SOME AI
1. In this course, AI tools may be used for ________________________.
2. They may not be used for ________________________, because that is the skill the assignment measures.
3. AI answers are not authoritative. When an AI tool disagrees with the syllabus or with me, the syllabus and I decide.
IF YOUR COURSE MOSTLY FORBIDS AI
1. In this course, AI tools may be used only for ________________________ (or: not at all, except ________________).
2. They may not be used to draft, outline, or write any work you submit, because the reading and writing are the skills this course builds.
3. If an assignment does not say, assume AI is not allowed. If you are unsure, ask me first; disclosed use is a conversation, undisclosed use is academic misconduct.
OPTIONAL DEFAULT LINE
If an assignment does not say, assume AI is not allowed. Where it is allowed, add one line: tool, what for, and how you checked it.

Questions people don't always ask out loud

  • Can I use my personal Gmail? No. ISU's guidance is to sign in with your Iowa State email and avoid the "free to the public" versions.
  • Does Google keep my handout? For Gemini signed in with your university account, ISU says "your interactions and uploaded content are private. They are not used to train models or shared with third parties."
  • Do textbook figures in my handout count? Publisher content you can't redistribute stays out. When in doubt, upload your own text and leave the figures.
  • Who sees the failure cards? Adisak types them up for the recap email without names or course numbers. Read aloud in the room only with your nod.
  • Is this "ISU-controlled infrastructure"? These are Google and Microsoft tools used under ISU's agreements, not servers ISU runs. ISU's guidance is what makes them the approved option.
  • Will the rules hold for 120 students? Not on their own. Prompts raise the odds; the tests are how you find out. Before students use a tutor, keep about 30 real questions and rerun them whenever you change a file.
  • Can student work go in? Not today. A tutor students use also turns their questions into student data. Ask ITS first.
