Part 0 · Start here

Our TREC 2026 in five minutes

TREC is a yearly evaluation run by the US National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST). Teams receive the same questions and the same collection, send in their system outputs, and NIST and the track organizers score them. In 2026 team iastate sent 55 runs to 10 tasks in 5 tracks.

What TREC is, in short

TREC stands for the Text REtrieval Conference. NIST has run it every year since 1992, and 2026 is the 35th edition. It is less a conference than a yearly exam for search and AI systems, followed by a workshop.

  • Why NIST? NIST is the US government's measurement agency. Its job is to measure things fairly and repeatably. In TREC it is the neutral referee: it does not compete, and it does the judging.
  • Why it is trusted. Every team gets the same documents and the same questions, and must submit before the answers exist. So nobody can tune a system to the test.
  • Why it matters. NIST reports that retrieval effectiveness about doubled in TREC's first six years. A 2010 economic study for NIST credited TREC-related work with about one third of the advances in web search between 1999 and 2009. Question answering at TREC is a direct ancestor of today's RAG track.
  • Why it matters to you. A student team answers the same questions as large research labs and appears in the same table. And in a time when AI answers sound right whether or not they are, careful evaluation is the scarce skill.

The full story is in Chapter 23.

What came back

RAG · retrieval

Seven of eight runs were scored. Our long-list run reached a set recall of 0.2927 against a track median of 0.1002, but it returned about 710 documents per narrative where the median list has 100. All our document sets were below the median on set precision.

Final. May be shown.

RAG · generation

Our two main runs used one generator, GPT-5 mini, with short and with long evidence. They were above the track median on the reported measures. Four runs that swapped the generator were at or below the median, and that comparison is not a fair model test. More scores are expected after the workshop.

Final. May be shown.

Video QA

Our fusion run placed first on Top-1 accuracy in the multiple-choice task. The fusion gained little over our best single model, and Chapter 37 explains why.

Final.

Change Detection

Seven runs without any large language model at run time. They were above the track median. The interesting part is the rule that decides when a question “fires”.

Final.
